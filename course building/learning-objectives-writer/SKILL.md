# Learning Objectives Writer

Produces 3–5 measurable, Bloom's-aligned learning objectives from a topic and instructor intent. Follow this sequence exactly.

---

## Step 1: Read context first

Before asking any questions, read the user's ABOUT ME folder or project context if available. Look for:
- Their discipline, institution, and course level
- Any prior objectives work or stated pedagogical preferences
- Patterns in how they describe student learning

Use what you find to reduce redundant questions. Don't ask for information they've already given you.

---

## Step 2: Gather inputs

Ask all five questions in a single grouped message. Do not send them one at a time.

```
To write your learning objectives, I need five things:

1. **Course and unit topic** — What course is this for, and what specific topic or
   unit are we writing objectives for?

2. **Student level and prior knowledge** — Who are the students? What do they
   already know or can already do coming into this unit?

3. **Target Bloom's level** — Where do you want students to end up by the end of
   this unit?
   - Remember — recall facts, terms, definitions
   - Understand — explain, interpret, summarize ideas
   - Apply — use knowledge in a new situation
   - Analyze — break down, compare, examine relationships
   - Evaluate — judge, critique, justify a position
   - Create — produce something new, design, synthesize

   You can name a level, describe it, or say "I'm not sure" and I'll recommend one.

4. **How students will demonstrate it** — What will students actually do to show
   they've learned? (e.g., write a paper, complete a lab, present, take a quiz,
   build something, discuss in class) Be rough if you're not sure — I'll work
   with what you give me.

5. **Time available** — How long is the unit? (days, weeks, class periods)
   This helps me scope the objectives realistically.
```

Wait for the instructor's response before proceeding.

---

## Step 3: Generate the objectives

Produce all five sections in a single response. Be direct and prescriptive — these are drafts the instructor will react to, not a menu of options.

---

### Section 1: Learning Objectives (3–5)

Write 3–5 objectives. Each must:
- Open with a **single measurable action verb** appropriate to the stated Bloom's level
- Specify the **content or skill** students are applying the verb to
- Include a **condition or context** where appropriate (e.g., "given a dataset," "using primary sources," "without reference materials")
- Be completable in the time available — do not write five objectives for a two-day unit

Format each as:

> **[Bloom's Level]** — Students will [verb] [content/skill] [condition if applicable].

Use the Bloom's verb lists below for verb selection. Do not invent verbs that sound measurable but aren't.

**Bloom's verb reference by level:**

| Level | Strong verbs |
|---|---|
| Remember | define, list, recall, identify, name, state, label, match |
| Understand | explain, summarize, paraphrase, classify, describe, interpret, give examples of |
| Apply | use, solve, demonstrate, execute, implement, calculate, complete, produce |
| Analyze | compare, contrast, differentiate, examine, break down, diagram, distinguish |
| Evaluate | critique, justify, argue, assess, defend, recommend, judge, prioritize |
| Create | design, construct, develop, compose, formulate, plan, produce, synthesize |

---

### Section 2: Evidence of Mastery

For each objective, write one sentence describing what observable student work would confirm the objective was met. This is not the assessment itself — it's the visible signal that the learning happened.

Format: bullet list, one sentence per objective, keyed to the objective number.

Keep these concrete. "Students can explain X clearly" is not evidence. "Students can explain X to a peer without notes in under two minutes" is.

---

### Section 3: Cut These First

Identify 2 of the objectives that should be cut if the instructor needs to trim scope. For each:
- **Which objective** (by number)
- **Why it's cuttable** — it's downstream of another objective, it's assessable elsewhere, or it's the least load-bearing for the unit's central purpose
- **What to do instead** — briefly note whether it belongs in a later unit, could become a stretch goal, or can simply go

Do not hedge. Name the cuts clearly.

---

### Section 4: Verb Traps to Avoid

List 4–6 verbs that are common in this discipline or at this level that fail the measurability test. For each:
- **The trap verb** — e.g., "understand," "know," "appreciate," "be familiar with," "learn about," "explore"
- **Why it fails** — you can't observe or assess it directly
- **What to use instead** — one or two measurable substitutes at the appropriate Bloom's level

Always include "understand," "know," and "appreciate" in this list. Add others that are specific to the discipline or level described.

---

### Section 5: A note on scope

In 2–4 sentences, assess whether the objectives as written are realistic for the time available. Flag any that are likely too ambitious. Recommend whether to narrow a single objective or drop one entirely if the scope is tight.

---

## Step 4: Offer next steps

After delivering the output, offer these follow-up options briefly:

1. **Revise** — adjust level, swap verbs, or reframe any objective
2. **Map to assessments** — suggest specific assessment tasks for each objective
3. **Full unit** — pass these objectives into the Course Blueprint Builder to build out the full sequence
